# Day 4 · R-Peak Detection and Heart-Rate Variability

> **NOT A MEDICAL DEVICE.** Every signal in this repo is generated by it.

A Pan-Tompkins-shaped detector built from Days 2 and 3: band-pass, square, integrate over a QRS width, threshold against the local level, then walk back to the tallest raw sample.

```
signal                 sens   prec   HR bpm   SDNN ms  RMSSD ms
ground truth              -      -     71.7      38.0      49.0
clean                  100%   100%     71.7      38.0      49.0
baseline wander        100%   100%     71.7      38.0      49.0
mains hum              100%   100%     71.7      38.2      50.1
sensor noise           100%   100%     71.7      37.9      48.5
motion artefact         92%    86%     76.8     297.1     381.1
```

Four of five are perfect, where Day 2's fixed threshold managed one. Motion artefact still breaks it — and look at *what* breaks: the heart rate is out by **7%** and the SDNN by **680%**.

## Heart rate is robust. Heart-rate variability is not.

```
error injected            beats   HR err   SDNN err  RMSSD err
none                         72     0.0%       0.0%       0.0%
1 beat(s) missed             71     1.4%     188.7%     214.7%
2 beat(s) missed             70     2.8%     284.4%     326.5%
4 beat(s) missed             68     5.6%     457.1%     537.8%
1 false beat(s)              73     1.4%     103.7%      70.7%
timing jitter +/-1           72     0.0%       0.1%       0.2%
timing jitter +/-3           72     0.0%       3.9%       8.0%
timing jitter +/-6           72     0.0%      15.0%      27.2%
```

**One missed beat out of seventy-two** — a detector at 98.6% sensitivity — costs 1.4% of the heart rate and 189% of the SDNN. An amplification of about **135×**.

The reason is arithmetic rather than physiology. Heart rate is a count over a duration, so losing one beat of seventy-two changes it by one part in seventy-two. HRV is the spread of the intervals, and a missed beat does not *shorten* an interval — it **merges two into one of double the length**, which is then far outside the distribution and contributes its error squared. A test asserts that mechanism directly, not just the symptom.

So a detector good enough to report a pulse is nowhere near good enough to report variability, **and the sensitivity figure does not tell you which you have.**

## Jitter matters; bias does not

```
every peak moved +10 samples: HR err 0.0%, SDNN err 0.0%, RMSSD err 0.0%
```

The heart rate is *exactly* right at every level of jitter, because the count has not changed. RMSSD is out by 27% at ±48 ms. And a constant offset costs **nothing at all** — HRV is computed from differences between intervals, so a bias cancels and only the variance of the error survives.

The specification for a peak detector feeding an HRV measure is not *accurate*. It is **precise**.

## So does the refinement step earn its place?

```
signal          variant               mean off   sd off   SDNN err  RMSSD err
clean           integrated only           1.00     0.00       0.0%       0.0%
clean           refined                   0.00     0.00       0.0%       0.0%
sensor noise    integrated only           1.11     2.35       5.3%       7.6%
sensor noise    refined                  -0.15     0.49       0.3%       1.4%
mains hum       integrated only           1.00     0.00       0.0%       0.0%
mains hum       refined                  -0.06     0.88       0.5%       2.2%
```

On the clean signal it removes a constant one-sample offset and changes the HRV by **nothing**, exactly as the section above predicts a bias would.

On sensor noise it cuts the offset's standard deviation from 2.35 samples to 0.49 and the RMSSD error from 7.6% to 1.4%. That is the case it was written for.

On mains hum it makes things **slightly worse** — the integrated signal is smooth and stable, while the raw signal still carries a 50 Hz ripple that moves the local maximum around.

The step is not a free win, and a detector that always refines is making an assumption about its noise. Tests pin all three directions.

## What Level 1 established

| Day | Finding |
|-----|---------|
| 1 | A signal with exact ground truth, because a public database's annotations are estimates too |
| 2 | SNR does not predict damage — at equal noise power, precision ran from 28% to 80% depending on the artefact |
| 3 | The filter costs what the beat needed, and two of three artefacts were better answered by a better detector |
| 4 | Heart rate survives a mediocre detector and variability does not, by a factor of about 135 |

The through-line is that **a number can be right for a reason that does not survive contact with the next measurement.**
