# Day 3 · Filtering: Moving Average, and What It Does to the Peaks

> **NOT A MEDICAL DEVICE.** Every signal in this repo is generated by it.

Three filters against Day 2's three artefacts. The recurring answer is that the filter costs something the beat needed.

## Removing baseline wander: the window is the whole design

```
 window s  R amp kept  wander left   sens   prec
      0.1         82%         0.0%   100%   100%
      0.2         90%         0.0%   100%   100%
      0.4         89%         0.1%   100%   100%
      0.6         91%         0.4%   100%   100%
      1.0         95%         2.4%   100%   100%
      2.0         93%        25.8%   100%   100%
```

Both ends of the trade are visible: 0.1 s keeps only 82% of the R peak because the average starts tracking the QRS itself; 2.0 s leaves a quarter of the wander behind. Anywhere between 0.4 and 1.0 s works — **the knob is not sharp**, which is the useful finding.

## Causal or centred: the delay a monitor cannot avoid

A centred window uses samples from the future. On a recording that is free; on a monitor the future has not happened yet.

```
 window  samples   centred   causal  causal ms  predicted
   0.02        5       0.0      2.0          8        2.0
   0.05       12      -1.0      4.0         16        5.5
   0.10       25      45.0     57.0        228       12.0
   0.20       50      22.0     46.0        184       24.5
```

Measured against `(w-1)/2` at windows where the peak survives, the agreement is **exact**:

```
   w  predicted  measured   R amp
   3        1.0       1.0     94%
   5        2.0       2.0     83%
   7        3.0       3.0     70%
   9        4.0       4.0     57%
  13        6.0       4.0     39%
  21       10.0       4.0     24%
```

Past w=9 the measured shift saturates while the prediction climbs — because the peak is under half its height and `argmax` has moved to a neighbouring feature. **A measurement whose subject has been destroyed does not return an error**, it returns a number. Two tests pin both halves.

## What smoothing costs the thing it was protecting

```
 window  R amp kept
   0.02         83%
   0.05         42%
   0.10         20%
   0.20         12%
```

A QRS is about 80 ms wide. A moving average approaching that width averages the peak against its own neighbours. There is no setting that removes high-frequency noise without touching the beat, because **the beat is the high-frequency content**.

## The notch that costs nothing, and why that is suspicious

```
                        energy 48-52  R amp kept   SNR dB
unfiltered                     52.0%        111%     -1.0
notch 1 Hz wide                 0.0%        100%     57.2
notch 5 Hz wide                 0.0%        100%     50.7
notch 20 Hz wide                0.0%        100%     39.8
```

The hum goes entirely, the R peak is untouched, and even a 20 Hz-wide notch costs nothing. That is too good, and the reason is a limitation of this repo rather than a fact about notch filters:

```
       band  share of the clean signal
     0-5 Hz                   45.7442%
    5-20 Hz                   43.2516%
   20-40 Hz                   10.9946%
   40-60 Hz                    0.0090%
   48-52 Hz                    0.0006%
```

The synthetic QRS holds **0.0006%** of its energy where the notch cuts, because Day 1 built it from smooth Gaussians. A real QRS has a sharp upstroke and meaningful content past 40 Hz, so a 50 Hz notch on real data does take something from the beat.

Day 1 said the waveform is a caricature. This is the first place the caricature changes an answer, and a test asserts the spectral fact so the caveat cannot be lost.

## Motion artefact, where filtering runs out

```
treatment                  sens   prec   R amp
none                       100%    57%    127%
remove baseline 0.6 s       96%    56%    118%
notch 50 Hz                100%    56%    126%
smooth 20 ms               100%    73%     94%
smooth 50 ms                25%    50%     49%
```

The two filters that fixed the other artefacts do nothing, because a burst has energy in every band. Mild smoothing buys sixteen points of precision; twice as much takes sensitivity from 100% to 25%.

There is no filter for motion artefact — only a narrow band of mild smoothing that helps a little before it starts deleting beats.

## And the fix for wander was not a filter at all

```
 wander mV  fixed sens  fixed prec  adapt sens  adapt prec
       0.0        100%        100%        100%        100%
       0.2        100%        100%        100%        100%
       0.4        100%         73%        100%        100%
       0.6        100%         53%         96%         88%
       0.8         79%         43%         62%         79%
       1.2         71%         36%         46%         42%
```

Comparing each sample against the **local** signal level instead of a fixed one takes precision from 73% back to 100%, with no filter, no window and no delay — exactly as Day 2 predicted, since wander moves the signal and not the timing.

**But only within a range.** Past 0.8 mV both detectors collapse, and the adaptive one's *sensitivity* is now the worse of the two, because the local maximum it compares against is itself inflated by the wander. It is not a strictly better detector; it is better over the range where wander is a nuisance rather than the dominant signal.

## Where this leads

Two of the three artefacts turned out to be detector problems rather than signal problems. **Day 4** builds the detector properly, and measures heart-rate variability — which needs the peak *positions* to be right, not just their count.
