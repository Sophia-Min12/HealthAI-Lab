# Day 2 · Noise: Baseline Wander, Mains Hum, Motion Artefact

> **NOT A MEDICAL DEVICE.** Every signal in this repo is generated by it.

Day 1 built a signal with exact ground truth. This adds the four things that ruin a real recording, and measures what each costs.

## Where each artefact lives

```
signal               SNR dB  dominant Hz  energy 5-20 Hz  energy >40 Hz
clean                   inf         1.20             43%             0%
baseline wander        -1.0         0.25             21%             0%
mains hum 50 Hz        14.6         1.20             42%             3%
sensor noise           19.6         1.20             43%             1%
motion artefact        -8.5         1.20             17%            57%
```

Wander pulls the dominant frequency down to its own 0.25 Hz and halves the share of energy in the QRS band. Hum and sensor noise leave the low band alone. **Motion artefact puts 57% of its energy above 40 Hz** — spread across the whole spectrum, which is what makes it resist filtering.

## What each costs a naive detector

`threshold_detect` is the obvious first idea: any sample above 0.5 mV, with a 200 ms refractory period. Nobody would ship it; it is here as an instrument.

```
signal              sensitivity  precision  found  true
clean                      100%       100%     24    24
baseline wander            100%        80%     30    24
mains hum 50 Hz            100%       100%     24    24
sensor noise               100%       100%     24    24
motion artefact            100%        57%     42    24
```

**Sensitivity never moves.** Noise adds energy, so the true peaks stay above the line — the detector does not *miss* beats under any of these. Every failure is a false positive, and reporting "the detector is 100% sensitive" would be true and useless.

## The measurement that matters: equal SNR, unequal damage

Comparing artefacts at equal amplitude compares units. So tune each until the signal-to-noise ratio is identical:

```
SNR = +0 dB
  artefact            amplitude  sensitivity  precision  found
  baseline wander         0.268         100%        80%     30
  mains hum               0.268         100%        50%     48
  sensor noise            0.190         100%        57%     42
  motion artefact         0.567         100%        66%     35

SNR = -5 dB
  baseline wander         0.476         100%        65%     37
  mains hum               0.476         100%        39%     62
  sensor noise            0.338         100%        28%     85
  motion artefact         1.008         100%        62%     39
```

At 0 dB — by construction the *same amount of noise power* — precision runs from **50% to 80%** depending only on which artefact. At −5 dB it runs from **28% to 65%**.

**SNR is out by more than a factor of two as a predictor of damage.**

And the ordering is not the intuitive one. Mains hum and sensor noise — the two a filter removes most easily — do **more** damage than motion artefact, which no filter can touch. A burst is confined to a few hundred milliseconds; hum is present at every sample, so it lifts the whole trace across the threshold over and over.

I had this backwards before the table was run. The `motion_artefact` docstring says so, and a test pins the direction: *hardest to filter* and *most damaging* are different questions with different answers.

## Wander moves the signal, not the timing

```
baseline wander at 0.4 mV: sensitivity 100%, precision 80%
```

Every true beat is still found, in the right place. What breaks is the fixed threshold, because the trace now drifts across it whether or not there is a beat.

That distinction decides Day 3's design: a detector comparing a sample against a **fixed** level is destroyed by wander; one comparing against the **local baseline** is not.
