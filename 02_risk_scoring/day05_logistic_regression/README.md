# Day 5 · Logistic Regression From Scratch, and Why the Coefficients Are Readable

> **NOT A MEDICAL DEVICE.** Every patient in this repo is generated by it.

A synthetic cohort with **known generating coefficients** — for the same reason Day 1 generated its own R peaks. On real data the fitted number *is* the answer and there is nothing to check it against.

The outcome is **sampled** from each patient's true risk, not thresholded at it. A 30% patient without the event is not a model error, and Day 8 depends on that distinction existing.

## Why the coefficients are readable

`exp(coefficient)` is the multiplier on the **odds**, holding the other features fixed:

```
smoker                     odds x 2.23
diabetic                   odds x 2.46
age_decades_over_50        odds x 1.73
```

No tree ensemble produces that sentence, and it is most of why logistic regression is still what clinical risk scores are made of.

But "the odds double" is heard as "the risk doubles", and those are the same statement only when the risk is small:

```
 baseline risk  odds ratio  risk ratio
            1%        2.23        2.20
            5%        2.23        2.10
           12%        2.23        1.94
           30%        2.23        1.63
           60%        2.23        1.28
```

The coefficient did not change. The sentence people hear did.

## What "readable" actually requires

Cholesterol rises with age in any real cohort. Make it do so here and refit — **the coefficients barely move**:

```
 correlation  r(age,chol)     age    chol     sum
         0.0        -0.00    0.58    0.29    0.86
         0.6         0.47    0.58    0.30    0.87
         2.0         0.87    0.56    0.30    0.87
        true                 0.55    0.30    0.85
```

I expected the model to split the shared signal arbitrarily between two correlated features. At twenty thousand patients it does not — **collinearity costs variance, not bias**, and twenty thousand patients is enough to pay it.

So draw the study again, and look at the spread:

```
     n      features    mean      sd     min     max
   300   independent    0.56    0.15    0.27    0.97
   300      r = 0.87    0.59    0.40   -0.41    1.36
  1000   independent    0.58    0.10
  1000      r = 0.87    0.60    0.21
  5000   independent    0.55    0.03
  5000      r = 0.87    0.58    0.08
```

The mean is right in every row. The standard deviation is **two to three times larger** under correlation, at every size.

And here is what that means for one study of three hundred patients:

```
features          coefficient range      odds ratio range
independent           0.27 to 0.97          1.31 to 2.64
r = 0.87             -0.41 to 1.36          0.66 to 3.90
the truth                     0.55                  1.73
```

Forty honest studies of the same population report age odds ratios from **0.66 to 3.90**. One of them concludes that age is *protective*.

The coefficient is still readable. What it is no longer is **reliable**, and nothing in the fitted number says which of those two studies you are holding.

## A numerical detail worth keeping

`sigmoid` returns exactly `1.0` from `z = 37` upward, because `1 + 2.2e-16` is `1.0` at double precision. Nothing is wrong with the arithmetic — the value has left the range float64 can distinguish from certainty.

That is why `log_loss` clips: one patient predicted at exactly 1.0 who has no event would make the mean over four thousand patients infinite. Tests pin the saturation point and the consequence.
